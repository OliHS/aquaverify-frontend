<h2 id="resumen-ejecutivo">Executive summary</h2>

- Excel may work at the beginning, but becomes fragile as samples, customers, lots, reviews and reports increase.
- A LIMS does not only store results: it connects request, sample, method, operator, review, CoA, customer portal and follow-up.
- Migration should start from the real workflow, not from the tool: intake, execution, review, report, stock and communication.
- AquaVerify Cloud connects CRM, LIMS, ELN, inventory, WMS, customer portal, dashboards and product workflows.
- The change should be phased: data inventory, minimum viable flow, critical traceability, reporting and scaling.

<h2 id="contenido-tecnico">Technical content</h2>

### When Excel stops being enough

The issue is not the spreadsheet itself, but fragmentation. When a sample lives in a table, the customer in a CRM, the lot in inventory, the report as a PDF and communication in email, the team loses context and review effort grows.

### What a water LIMS should cover

A water workflow needs sampling points, matrices, intake, worksheets, methods, controls, results, review, CoA, customer portal and history. When AquaVerify products are involved, the system should also connect demand, stock, lot and support.

### Migration by phases

The best migration does not try to digitize everything in one day. It starts with critical samples and results, adds review and reports, connects the customer portal and then expands to inventory, tasks, support and dashboards.

<h2 id="faq">FAQ</h2>

### Does a LIMS replace every current system?

Not necessarily. The priority is to connect the critical flow: sample, result, review, report, portal and follow-up.

### Where should teams start?

With the flow that creates the most risk or administrative time: sample intake, CoA release or lot traceability.

### Is AquaVerify Cloud only a LIMS?
